### P1 — Motivation and primary literature
Balinese Endek weaving is a traditional Indonesian textile practice whose continuity depends on the transmission of artisanal knowledge to younger generations. In May 2026, the Provincial Government of Bali expressed concern about declining participation among young weavers, underscoring the relevance of cultural documentation and education [3]. Generative visual models provide new means of exploring textile design, yet images intended for repeated textile layouts must satisfy constraints that extend beyond local photographic plausibility. In particular, visible discontinuities across opposite borders can undermine the usefulness of an otherwise realistic motif when it is repeated in a fabric pattern. He *et al.* [1] address the generation of tileable textures for cloth digitization through intrinsic priors, including patch swapping and patch tiling. Their work establishes a relevant textile-specific foundation for analyzing repeatability and boundary continuity. However, that framework uses an image texture as a conditioning reference rather than an open-ended description of the desired motif.

### P2 — Gap and secondary same-task literature
Text-driven synthesis introduces a complementary challenge: image content must respond to a linguistic description while retaining visual realism. Tao *et al.* [2] developed DF-GAN, a one-stage text-to-image generative adversarial model using deep text–image fusion and a target-aware discriminator. Unlike tileability-oriented texture methods, its original design does not specifically optimize opposing-edge consistency for periodic textile repeats. Thus, TiPGAN [1] and DF-GAN [2] address different aspects of the desired application, while neither original model directly demonstrates the joint setting of Indonesian-language motif prompting and periodic border continuity for source-audited Balinese Endek images. This is a difference in task scope, not evidence that either original method fails on its own benchmark.

### P3 — Present system, methodology, and evidence
This study investigates Endek T2I, a multilingual text- and motif-class-conditioned GAN incorporating border-consistency regularization and a source-grouped preprocessing protocol. The evaluated 256 × 256-pixel dataset contains 1,280 preprocessed training records in the main arm and 37 records each for validation and held-out testing; the training records derive from 296 independent source images rather than 1,280 distinct photographs. We conduct three-seed, source-matched ablations of boundary regularization and augmentation/cropping, with a DF-GAN architecture adapted to the same frozen MiniLM text features as a text-input comparator. Preliminary results reveal a measurable trade-off: boundary discontinuity decreases when seam regularization is applied, while distributional quality and perceptual diversity generally deteriorate on the limited test set. The image-conditioned TiPGAN is treated as a distinct texture-synthesis reference rather than assigned invalid text-prompt scores. The present study is therefore framed as a controlled assessment of this fidelity–tileability trade-off, not as proof of a newly invented seam loss or expert-certified cultural authenticity.
