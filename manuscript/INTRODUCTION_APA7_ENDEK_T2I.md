# 1. INTRODUCTION

*Working manuscript draft for IEEE Access. Citations and the reference list follow APA 7th edition for reference-manager editing; IEEE numerical citations will be needed in the final submission. The scientific method is named Endek T2I, without a version suffix.*

Balinese Endek is a traditional Indonesian woven textile whose ornamental vocabulary, production techniques, and regional identity depend on knowledge transmitted across generations of artisans. Its continuity requires more than preserving digital photographs: the craft relies on sustained weaving practice, contextual understanding of motifs, and opportunities for younger people to engage with the design process. A participatory study of Endek production in Jinengdalem Village demonstrated how locally inspired motifs, natural dyes, and collaboration with weaving communities can support sustainable design practices (Arfan et al., 2024). More recently, the Provincial Government of Bali expressed concern about declining interest among younger generations in becoming Endek weavers despite continued demand for the fabric, identifying a threat to intergenerational craftsmanship rather than establishing that the tradition itself has disappeared (Provincial Government of Bali, 2026). This motivates digital tools that can document visual structures and facilitate motif exploration for learners, designers, and researchers. Text-to-image (T2I) generation is potentially useful because a user can describe motif categories and visual attributes in natural language and examine corresponding synthetic design alternatives. Nevertheless, generated imagery should be understood as educational and exploratory support, not as proof of cultural authenticity or direct feasibility on a traditional loom.

Computational generation of Indonesian heritage textiles is an established but still developing research direction. Hendrawati et al. (2024) investigated fine-tuned Stable Diffusion for Balinese Endek designs, reporting measures of image–text similarity and establishing the feasibility of prompt-driven motif exploration. Purnawan and Suparjana (2026) subsequently evaluated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for Endek generation using a separately curated dataset of 687 textile images assigned to geometric, flora, fauna, and decorative classes. Research on another Indonesian textile, Batik, has also applied prompt-conditioned latent diffusion, LoRA, and classifier-free guidance, illustrating the relevance of descriptive conditioning and domain-specific design evaluation (Wahid et al., 2025). Such studies demonstrate that Endek and other cultural motifs have already been investigated using generative artificial intelligence; the present study does not claim to be the first. However, the reported datasets, training procedures, and evaluation conditions differ, and improvements in visual realism or prompt similarity do not independently establish periodic continuity when an image is repeated as a textile pattern. In addition, augmented or cropped images derived from a small number of photographs should not be mistaken for independent observations of craft diversity. A focused investigation is therefore needed to distinguish the effects of preprocessing, motif conditioning, and edge continuity under explicit source-aware evaluation.

Tileability presents a distinct technical requirement because opposite edges must join without conspicuous discontinuities when motifs are repeated. Rodriguez-Pardo and Garces (2023) developed SeamlessGAN to synthesize tileable texture maps from an exemplar image through latent-space tiling and adversarial learning. Of particular relevance to cloth digitization, He et al. (2025) proposed TiPGAN, integrating a texture-style encoder, Patch Swapping, and Patch Tiling with a Relative Total Variation (RTV) metric for periodic texture continuity. **TiPGAN serves as the primary methodological reference** in the present study because its explicit treatment of textile tileability offers a more specific point of departure than generic image synthesis. Its published task, however, is conditioned on image texture exemplars rather than open-ended Indonesian-language instructions describing an Endek motif category, color palette, or composition. That difference is a limitation of task coverage, not evidence of deficient tileability performance by TiPGAN. Extending the research question toward text-guided motif generation therefore requires evaluating boundary continuity together with conditional image synthesis, while recognizing that an image-conditioned reference method cannot fairly be scored for adherence to text prompts it does not accept.

Text-conditioned generative adversarial networks provide a complementary basis for comparison. Tao et al. (2022) introduced DF-GAN, a one-stage architecture with deep text–image fusion and a target-aware discriminator that strengthens correspondence between linguistic descriptions and synthetic images. It forms the **secondary external methodological comparator** because its original T2I formulation does not explicitly optimize agreement between the opposing borders of periodic textile patterns. Limited independent training samples also create an important evaluation constraint: Karras et al. (2020) showed that generative adversarial learning can suffer from discriminator overfitting when the training collection is small and investigated adaptive augmentation as a mitigation strategy. Although preprocessing can increase the number of image views seen during training, it cannot increase the number of independent source photographs. The resulting research gap is not a claim that the components of conditional generation or seamless texture synthesis are individually unknown. Rather, it concerns the controlled measurement of their interaction for source-grouped Endek data: **can text-conditioned motif synthesis improve boundary continuity without unacceptable losses in distributional fidelity and perceptual diversity, and what changes when source-aware crop augmentation is introduced?**

To investigate this question, this study evaluates **Endek T2I**, a text- and motif-class-conditioned generative framework using a frozen multilingual text representation, learned class embeddings, conditional adversarial synthesis, and opposing-border regularization. Its experimental contribution is a source-aware preprocessing protocol combined with paired ablations of crop augmentation and seam regularization, rather than a claim that text conditioning or seam penalties have been newly invented. In the principal preprocessed training arm, **1,280 training records** comprise 944 full-frame or augmented views and 336 additional crop views, all derived from **296 independent training images**; validation and held-out testing each comprise 37 other independent source images. The study completed **24 internal training runs** across seeds 42, 123, and 2025 and evaluated six matched held-out runs of the no-seam control and seam-regularized configuration using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), perceptual diversity, and an opposing-edge discontinuity metric. Across the three seeds, mean edge gap declined from **0.08993 to 0.05338 (40.65%)** with seam regularization, whereas mean pooled FID increased from **254.47 to 302.13** and mean KID increased from **0.12848 to 0.19085**, demonstrating a **fidelity–tileability trade-off** rather than an overall performance advantage. These measurements are exploratory because the independent held-out test contains only 37 images; the inherited prompt descriptions, particularly those of cropped views, also await independent semantic validation and evaluation by Endek experts. The resulting contribution is thus a reproducible examination of preprocessing and boundary objectives for culturally contextualized T2I motif synthesis, with explicit separation between completed internal comparisons and the still-incomplete external TiPGAN and adapted DF-GAN–MiniLM benchmarks.
