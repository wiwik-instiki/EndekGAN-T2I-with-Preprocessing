# 1. INTRODUCTION

Balinese Endek is a traditional Indonesian woven textile distinguished by patterned motifs and craft knowledge sustained through local weaving communities. Beyond its aesthetic value, Endek embodies knowledge about motif composition and production that is learned through practice and transmitted between generations. Research undertaken with weaving communities in Jinengdalem has highlighted the value of participatory design and locally grounded innovation in supporting the long-term continuity of Endek production (Arfan et al., 2024). The Provincial Government of Bali has also warned that declining interest among younger people in weaving threatens the regeneration of artisans, even as Endek remains visible in contemporary use (Provincial Government of Bali, 2026). These concerns call for educational and design-support tools that can broaden access to motif knowledge while respecting the role of artisans. Text-to-image (T2I) generation can contribute to such efforts by converting verbal descriptions into visual design candidates. For traditional textiles, however, successful generation requires more than an attractive image: a motif should reflect the requested visual attributes and maintain coherence when its pattern is repeated.

Generative artificial intelligence has begun to support motif design for Indonesian textiles. Hendrawati et al. (2024) applied fine-tuned Stable Diffusion to Balinese Endek motif generation and evaluated synthetic designs using image–text similarity. Purnawan and Suparjana (2026) investigated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for Endek pattern synthesis across four motif categories. In a related textile domain, Wahid et al. (2025) employed LoRA-weighted diffusion with classifier-free guidance to generate Batik patterns from textual prompts. These studies establish the feasibility of language-guided exploration of cultural textile motifs. Two requirements nevertheless warrant more focused investigation. First, visual plausibility or correspondence with a prompt does not establish whether opposite image borders join consistently when a motif is tiled. Second, preprocessing can create numerous derivative images from a smaller collection of originals, making source provenance essential to interpreting model evaluation. Both requirements are relevant to domain-specific image collections, where repeated views may increase training exposure without increasing the diversity of independent source motifs.

Research on texture synthesis addresses periodic continuity more directly. Rodriguez-Pardo and Garces (2023) developed SeamlessGAN to generate repeatable texture maps from image exemplars. He et al. (2025) subsequently introduced TiPGAN, a cloth-oriented texture synthesis model that employs Patch Swapping and Patch Tiling to promote seamless repetition. TiPGAN serves as the primary methodological reference for the present study because it places tileability at the center of textile image generation. Its formulation, however, is conditioned on an image exemplar rather than on a natural-language description of the desired Endek motif. By contrast, Tao et al. (2022) proposed DF-GAN, which combines deep text–image fusion with a target-aware discriminator for text-conditioned image synthesis. DF-GAN therefore serves as the supporting reference for language-guided generation, although its original formulation does not explicitly enforce consistency between opposing borders of a repeated textile pattern. These approaches offer complementary foundations for visual repeatability and text conditioning, but neither directly characterizes their interaction under a source-controlled protocol for Balinese Endek motif generation.

A further methodological challenge concerns the relationship between preprocessing and independent visual evidence. Training generative adversarial networks with restricted image diversity can encourage discriminator overfitting, motivating augmentation strategies that improve data efficiency (Karras et al., 2020). Nevertheless, augmented images and cropped motif fragments remain derived observations; they should not be treated as new independent sources or allowed to cross the boundaries between training, validation, and testing. The research gap addressed here is therefore the absence, within the selected methodological references, of a controlled assessment that separates the effects of source-aware crop augmentation from the effects of seam regularization in text-guided Endek generation. This study formulates two research questions. **RQ1:** How does source-aware preprocessing, particularly the inclusion of crop-derived training views, influence the validation quality of text-guided Endek motif synthesis when the independent source partitions are held fixed? **RQ2:** How does opposing-border regularization affect motif tileability, and what trade-offs does it introduce in image fidelity and perceptual diversity? These questions connect the data-preparation strategy and the generative objective to distinct, testable aspects of the synthesis process.

To address these questions, this study presents **Endek T2I**, a text- and motif-class-conditioned generative framework incorporating source-aware preprocessing and an opposing-border consistency objective. The **main contribution** is a controlled methodological framework that connects image-source provenance, language-guided motif generation, and periodic texture constraints within a reproducible experimental design. Three specific contributions support this objective. First, an independent-source partitioning and class-balanced preprocessing procedure combines full-image augmentation with crop-derived training views while preserving separate validation and test sources; this component provides the basis for addressing RQ1. Second, a conditional generative formulation combines multilingual text features and motif-class information with seam-consistency regularization to investigate repeatable Endek motif synthesis. Third, a matched evaluation protocol uses preprocessing and regularization ablations, repeated training, and complementary assessments of image fidelity, perceptual diversity, and boundary continuity to determine the effects associated with RQ2. Together, these contributions provide a systematic basis for assessing text-guided Endek motif generation and for informing future digital design exploration and educational resources concerning Balinese textile heritage.
