# 2. RELATED WORK

## 2.1. Text-Guided Generation of Traditional Textile Motifs

Text-to-image (T2I) models provide a means of exploring textile motifs through descriptions of visual attributes, including ornament categories, colors, and geometric arrangements. In Balinese Endek research, Hendrawati et al. (2024) investigated fine-tuned Stable Diffusion for generating motifs from textual descriptions, using CLIP-based similarity to assess the correspondence between images and prompts. This work established an application-oriented foundation for Endek generation, although semantic similarity alone does not measure whether a synthesized pattern can be repeated without visible edge discontinuities. Purnawan and Suparjana (2026) subsequently investigated Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for fine-tuning a diffusion model on 687 Endek images spanning geometric, flora, fauna, and decorative motif classes. Their evaluation incorporated Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), Inception Score, and CLIP similarity, demonstrating the value of examining both visual properties and textual conditioning. However, the reported aggregate quality metrics do not by themselves establish source-independent generalization or periodic agreement between opposite motif boundaries.

Work on related Indonesian textiles offers complementary perspectives on generative control. Wahid et al. (2025) combined a LoRA-weighted latent diffusion model with classifier-free guidance for prompt-conditioned Batik pattern generation, emphasizing controllable synthesis of culturally specific designs. A more recent study of indigenous Dayak motifs applied a taxonomy-guided prompt framework grounded in cultural descriptors and evaluated generated images using a blinded expert panel (Hatef Jalil & Abdullah, 2026). The latter is especially relevant to the distinction between visual plausibility and cultural interpretation: automatically generated imagery requires domain-appropriate assessment before claims about symbolic accuracy or heritage authenticity can be sustained. These studies provide useful precedents for Endek T2I but do not eliminate the need to evaluate preprocessing provenance and repeat-pattern consistency separately. Text–image alignment, cultural appropriateness, and geometric tileability remain different evaluation dimensions.

## 2.2. Seamless Texture Synthesis and Boundary Continuity

Texture synthesis research has addressed the spatial consistency required when a generated image is repeated over a larger surface. Rodriguez-Pardo and Garces (2023) proposed SeamlessGAN, a self-supervised approach that generates tileable texture maps from an input exemplar through a generative representation designed for seamless repetition. For cloth digitization, He et al. (2025) introduced TiPGAN, which employs intrinsic texture priors, a Patch Swapping Module, and a Patch Tiling Module to improve continuity across adjacent texture tiles. These design choices make TiPGAN the principal textile-specific methodological reference for the present study. Its publicly released formulation is nevertheless image-conditioned: a reference texture provides the appearance to be synthesized, whereas an Endek T2I system is expected to respond to a natural-language description and a motif category. This difference concerns the conditioning interface rather than the effectiveness of TiPGAN on its intended texture-generation task.

Recent diffusion-based work also demonstrates that the fidelity–tileability problem is not unique to adversarial models. Zheng et al. (2026) proposed DiCoT, a dual-scale dynamic-context method for seamless textile generation that combines window scheduling with padding strategies to balance broad periodic structure and local texture detail. Therefore, enforcing seamlessness while retaining visual quality is an established technical direction, rather than a capability that this study claims to introduce for the first time. What remains pertinent to Endek T2I is how an explicitly regularized border objective behaves in a text-conditioned, class-aware generative setting when preprocessing choices and independent source partitions are controlled. Image-conditioned methods such as TiPGAN can inform a separate tileability comparison, but their outputs cannot be judged using a text-prompt adherence criterion that the models do not receive.

## 2.3. Conditional GANs, Preprocessing, and Evaluation Integrity

Conditional generative adversarial networks offer an alternative to diffusion-based T2I generation. Tao et al. (2022) proposed DF-GAN, a one-stage framework that integrates text–image features and uses a target-aware discriminator to support correspondence between generated images and linguistic descriptions. Its original architecture does not explicitly impose a periodic boundary-consistency constraint. Accordingly, DF-GAN is selected as the supporting text-conditioned methodological reference, distinct from the image-conditioned TiPGAN. In the present research protocol, the external DF-GAN implementation is adapted to a frozen multilingual MiniLM text representation; it should therefore be identified as an adapted DF-GAN–MiniLM comparator, not described as an exact reproduction of the original text-encoding pipeline.

The treatment of training data is equally important to an interpretable comparison. Karras et al. (2020) examined generative adversarial learning under restricted training-data availability and showed why augmentation can be important for preventing discriminator overfitting. Augmentation, however, addresses training exposure rather than the independence of the underlying images. Kapoor and Narayanan (2023) documented how data leakage and weaknesses in experimental design can undermine claims of machine-learning reproducibility. For textile images, multiple augmented views or crops originating from one photograph must consequently remain in the same partition; otherwise, visually related samples could inflate an apparently independent evaluation. Even where exact file-level duplicates have been removed, near-duplicate imagery and inherited text descriptions warrant separate checks. This distinction motivates evaluating the addition of crop-derived training views under fixed source-grouped partitions, rather than claiming that preprocessing has increased the number of independently acquired motifs.

## 2.4. Research Positioning and Comparison With Existing Methods

The reviewed literature addresses different portions of the Endek-generation problem. Endek- and Batik-focused diffusion studies establish domain adaptation and prompt-guided synthesis; texture-oriented generators target seamless repetition; conditional GANs provide a text-conditioned adversarial architecture; and data-integrity research motivates source-aware evaluation. Table I summarizes the methodological scope of the closest studies and clarifies their relationship to the present work.

**TABLE I. Methodological comparison of related textile-generation and source-aware evaluation studies.**

| Study | Primary conditioning / method | Main investigated dimension | Relevance and distinction for Endek T2I |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hendrawati et al. (2024) | Text; fine-tuned Stable Diffusion on Endek | Prompt-related motif generation | Establishes prior Endek T2I research; explicit seam and source-group ablations are not established by the reported evaluation |
| Purnawan and Suparjana (2026) | Text; LoRA-tuned diffusion on Endek | Image quality, perceptual similarity, prompt correspondence | Close domain-specific prior work; different dataset and architecture, so published metric values are not directly comparable |
| Wahid et al. (2025) | Text; LoRA-weighted diffusion and classifier-free guidance on Batik | Controllable motif generation | Relevant Indonesian textile analogue, but not an Endek-specific periodic-border experiment |
| Hatef Jalil and Abdullah (2026) | Structured vernacular prompts; diffusion-based indigenous motifs | Cultural and morphological evaluation by experts | Demonstrates a complementary cultural-validation requirement, not a seam-controlled Endek benchmark |
| Rodriguez-Pardo and Garces (2023) | Image exemplar; SeamlessGAN | Tileable texture synthesis | Establishes exemplar-based periodic texture generation rather than Endek prompt conditioning |
| **He et al. (2025), TiPGAN** | **Reference image; patch swapping and tiling** | **Seamless cloth-oriented texture synthesis** | **Primary methodological reference; image-conditioned tileability comparison requires a distinct protocol** |
| Zheng et al. (2026), DiCoT | Diffusion with dynamic windows and phase-aware padding | Joint texture fidelity and seamless repetition | Shows recent seamless-textile advances; not an executed Endek T2I baseline |
| **Tao et al. (2022), DF-GAN** | **Text; deep text–image fusion and target-aware discriminator** | **Text-conditioned GAN image synthesis** | **Secondary methodological reference; adapted MiniLM-based implementation is the external T2I comparator** |

*Note.* The table distinguishes a paper's reported emphasis from capabilities that were not explicitly established in the reviewed evidence. It does not imply that an unreported assessment is impossible or that all listed models were trained on the same dataset.

As indicated in Table I, no single selected reference simultaneously establishes a source-grouped preprocessing ablation and an opposing-border regularization ablation for text- and class-conditioned Balinese Endek motif synthesis. This is a bounded gap in the evaluated literature, not a claim that conditional generation, crop augmentation, or seam regularization is individually novel. The previously developed EndekGAN-T2I research implementation already incorporated text conditioning and a seam penalty; Endek T2I therefore does not present those components as first-time inventions. Instead, the present study investigates their behavior within a controlled preprocessing and evaluation framework. Specifically, the source-aware full-frame versus crop-augmented comparison provides the design required to address RQ1, whereas the matched no-seam versus seam-regularized comparison evaluates tileability together with fidelity and perceptual diversity for RQ2. This positioning leads to the experimental framework and proposed method described in the next section.
